Quiet activities for a 3-year-old on a plane or car ride

Hop Along Books · 2026-10-08

A long flight or drive with a 3-year-old goes better with a small bag of things to do. Here is what tends to work, and a few tricks that make each activity last longer.

Pack several small things, not one big thing

Attention spans at 3 are short. Five activities that each hold attention for ten minutes beat one that is supposed to last an hour. Bring them out one at a time and put each away before the next comes out.

Coloring that works on a tray table

  • Crayons, not markers. No caps to lose, no stains on the seat, no bleed-through.
  • Fewer crayons. A small pouch with the 6 to 10 colors the page needs is easier than a full box that spills.
  • Big, simple pictures. Bumpy roads and small tray tables make fine coloring hard. Pages with big spaces and only a few colors are easier to finish on the move.
  • Tear out a page or two before you leave so a whole book isn't sliding around.

Other quiet favorites

  • Sticker books and reusable sticker scenes
  • A few small, new toys wrapped in paper, opened one at a time
  • Picture books to read together, or look-and-find books
  • Snacks in a container with several small compartments, which doubles as an activity

Games that need nothing

I spy with colors ("I spy something blue"), counting cars or clouds, and guessing animal sounds. These are good for the last half hour, when everything in the bag has been used.

Keep one thing in reserve

Save one new activity for the hardest moment: the delay, the descent, or the last stretch of the drive.
